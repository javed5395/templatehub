Set 01 · Profile & Banner · LinkedIn

The headline, rewritten to be found.

Your LinkedIn headline is 220 characters that appear next to your name in every search result, every comment, every connection request. By default it is just your job title, which means you are competing with everyone who shares it. Recruiters search by keyword, and the headline is weighted heavily. Three samples: mid-career, a career changer, and someone actively looking.

Sample OneEmployed · Not actively looking

Three versions of the same person, getting progressively more useful. Notice how the search terms increase without the line becoming a keyword list.

Bilal Ahmed
Senior Accountant · Lahore
The default (what LinkedIn writes for you)34 chars
Senior Accountant at Nishat Textile Mills
This is what appears if you never touch it. It contains one searchable term — "accountant" — which you share with roughly forty thousand people in Pakistan.
Better118 chars
Senior Accountant | Month-End Close, Payables & Tax Compliance | ACCA Part Qualified | Nishat Textile Mills
Four searchable terms now: month-end close, payables, tax compliance, ACCA. A recruiter filtering for any of them finds you.
Best204 chars
Senior Accountant | Cut month-end close 12 days → 6 across three entities | Payables, reconciliation & tax compliance | ACCA finalist | SAP FICO | Lahore
Same keywords, plus one result and one system. The number stops the scroll; the keywords make you findable in the first place. Both jobs, one line.
Why the arrow works
12 days → 6 It reads faster than "reduced from twelve days to six" and survives being truncated on mobile, where roughly the first 60 characters show. Put the number early enough that it survives the cut.
Why this one works
  • Keywords are the whole mechanism. Recruiter search runs on terms. "Month-end close" and "reconciliation" are what a finance recruiter actually types; "detail-oriented" is not.
  • One number earns its space. Twelve to six. It is the only part that distinguishes him from other accountants who list the same skills.
  • The system is named. SAP FICO is a hard filter in finance recruitment and takes eight characters.
  • The city is included. Location is a common search filter and the headline is a cheap place to satisfy it.
  • The employer moved to the end or dropped entirely. It already appears under your name — repeating it wastes characters you could spend on being found.
Sample TwoMoving between fields

The hardest headline. You need to be found for the field you are entering while being honest about where your experience is.

Hina Rauf
Teacher moving into corporate training · Lahore
Wrong — leads with the past52 chars
Senior Mathematics Teacher at Beaconhouse School System
Accurate, and it guarantees she is only ever found by schools. The headline is a search term, not a job description.
Wrong — leads with the wish71 chars
Aspiring L&D Professional | Passionate about corporate learning | Open to opportunities
"Aspiring" and "passionate" are not search terms and they signal that she has not done it yet. Nobody searches for aspiring anything.
Right196 chars
Learning & Development | Onboarding curriculum for 60 engineers, assessment pass rates 68% → 91% | Instructional design, facilitation, cohort management | 7 yrs teaching background
Leads with the destination field and the evidence for it. The teaching background is stated at the end as context, not as an apology.
The principle
Lead with where you are going, evidenced. Mention where you came from, briefly, at the end. Never use "aspiring", "transitioning", "seeking to move into", or "passionate about". They all say the same thing: I have not done this yet.
Why this one works
  • The field she wants comes first. "Learning & Development" is the term an L&D recruiter searches. Leading with "Teacher" removes her from every one of those searches.
  • The evidence is immediate. Sixty engineers, 68% to 91%. It answers "has she actually done this?" in the same breath as the claim.
  • Her real skills are named in the field's own vocabulary. Instructional design, facilitation, cohort management — all searchable, all true.
  • Seven years teaching is context, not apology. Four words at the end. It explains the shape of her profile without leading with it.
Sample ThreeUnemployed or openly looking

When you are looking openly, the headline has an extra job — it must say so without making that the entire identity.

Nadia Aslam
Finance Manager, returning after a career break · Karachi
Wrong42 chars
Seeking new opportunities | Open to work
It says nothing about what you do. A recruiter cannot search for this and would not know what to do with you if they found it.
Right211 chars
Finance Manager | ACCA | Month-end close, IFRS reporting & audit for PKR 12B groups | Cut close 12 days → 6 | Available from October | Karachi
Availability is one clause among five, not the headline itself. Everything before it is what makes her findable.
Use the Open To Work setting instead
LinkedIn has a built-in "Open to work" setting with two visibility options — recruiters only, or the green photo frame visible to everyone. Recruiters-only is the better default. It puts you in the recruiter search pool without announcing it to your current employer or your network. The green frame is worth using when you are openly unemployed and want the reach.
One line for the break
Career break 2023–2025 (family care) — ACCA completed during this period Put this in the About section, not the headline. Stating it somewhere visible stops it being a question; putting it in the headline makes it the first thing anyone learns about you.
Why this one works
  • Availability is one clause, not the message. "Available from October" sits fifth. Everything ahead of it is what gets her found.
  • Scale is named. "PKR 12B groups" tells a recruiter immediately whether she is the right size, which is the second filter after keywords.
  • The break is handled in About, not the headline. Visible, factual, one line — and not the first thing a stranger reads.
  • Recruiters-only visibility is the safe default. Full search visibility without exposing the search to your employer.

How recruiters actually search

LinkedIn Recruiter is a keyword search tool. A recruiter filling a finance role types terms — "month-end close", "IFRS", "ACCA", "SAP" — filters by location and years of experience, and works down a list. That is the entire mechanism.

The headline is weighted heavily in that search, and it is also the only text that follows you everywhere: search results, comments, connection requests, message previews. It is the most reused 220 characters you own.

Which means the question is not "what is my job title" but "what would someone type if they were looking for a person like me". Write those words down. Those are your headline.

The formula

Five parts, separated by pipes, in this order:

  • Role or target role. The searchable title, not your internal grade.
  • Two to four specialisms. The exact terms a recruiter would type.
  • One number. Your strongest result, written short with an arrow.
  • A qualification or system, if it is a filter in your field — ACCA, PMP, SAP, AWS, PEC, PNC.
  • City, if you want local roles.

Then check it on a phone. Roughly the first 60 characters show before truncation in most views, so the role and your single best term must live in that space.

And avoid: "results-driven", "passionate", "detail-oriented", "team player", "guru", "ninja", and emoji clutter. None of them are searched for and all of them cost characters.

The rest of the profile

The headline is the highest-leverage field but four others matter for search, and all four are commonly left as defaults.

  • Job titles in your experience section. If your internal title is "Officer Grade II", write the searchable equivalent — recruiters do not search for internal grades.
  • Skills. Up to 50, and they are directly searchable. Fill them, and pin the three most relevant.
  • The About section. Indexed, and the first two lines show before "see more".
  • Your custom URL. linkedin.com/in/yourname rather than a string of digits. It goes on your resume.

One caution: everything on LinkedIn is visible to your current employer, and profile updates can generate notifications. Turn off "share profile changes" in settings before a rewrite if your search is confidential.
