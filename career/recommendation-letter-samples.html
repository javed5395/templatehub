Set 01 · Recommendation · Recommendation Letters

Professional recommendation, with evidence.

Most recommendation letters are warm, generous and completely useless, because they contain only adjectives. A reader who has seen fifty of them discounts praise entirely and looks for specifics — what this person did, how much of it, and what happened. Two specifics beat ten adjectives. Three samples: a manager writing for a direct report, a peer, and the version you draft yourself when asked to.

Sample OneFour years managed · Strong recommendation

The standard case. Notice how little of it is praise and how much is simply what happened, stated precisely.

Kashif Nadeem
Finance Manager, Nishat Textile Mills  ·  k.nadeem@nishatmills.com  ·  +92 300 4471 118
12 August 2026
To Whom It May Concern
Letter of Recommendation — Mr. Bilal Ahmed
Dear Sir/Madam,

I have managed Bilal Ahmed directly for four years, from April 2022 until the present, in his role as Senior Accountant at Nishat Textile Mills. I am writing this without reservation and at his request.

Bilal owns the month-end close for three of our entities. When he took it on, close took twelve working days. It now takes six. He achieved that not by working longer hours but by standardising our intercompany reconciliation, which was the actual bottleneck and which three people before him had not identified.

In 2023 he ran a reconciliation audit on his own initiative and recovered PKR 3.2 million in duplicate vendor payments. Nobody asked him to look. He has also filed three years of sales and income tax returns without a single penalty, which in my experience is unusual.

He supervises two accounts officers. One of them was promoted last year, largely on the strength of how Bilal had developed her. He is the person I give work to when I need it to be correct rather than fast, and he is straightforward about what he does not know, which I value more than confidence.

I am sorry to lose him and would re-employ him immediately. I am happy to be contacted directly about any of the above.

Yours faithfully,
Kashif Nadeem
Kashif Nadeem · Finance Manager · Nishat Textile Mills · k.nadeem@nishatmills.com
Why this one works
  • The relationship and duration open the letter. "Managed directly for four years" establishes how much weight the reader should give everything that follows.
  • The central claim has a mechanism. Twelve days to six, and why — the intercompany reconciliation. A result without a mechanism reads as luck.
  • "Nobody asked him to look." Four words that demonstrate initiative more convincingly than the word "proactive" ever could.
  • The praise is qualified. "Correct rather than fast" and "straightforward about what he does not know". A letter with no shape at all reads as generic; a specific characterisation reads as true.
  • It ends with a concrete claim. "I would re-employ him immediately" is the strongest closing line available and it costs the writer something, which is why it lands.
Sample TwoCross-functional colleague

A peer cannot speak to how someone performs under management, so the letter should be honest about that and concentrate on what a colleague actually sees.

Sadia Rehman
Internal Audit Manager, Nishat Textile Mills  ·  s.rehman@nishatmills.com  ·  +92 321 7719 004
12 August 2026
To Whom It May Concern
Letter of Recommendation — Mr. Bilal Ahmed
Dear Sir/Madam,

I have worked alongside Bilal Ahmed for four years. I am not his manager — I run internal audit and he runs the entity closes, so we sit on opposite sides of the same process. That is the perspective this letter offers.

Across three annual audits, Bilal has been the person in finance whose working papers did not need to be sent back. In 2024 and 2025 his sections were signed off without a single follow-up query, which was true of nobody else in the department.

When I raised a control weakness in the payment approval process in 2023, he did not defend it, which is the usual reaction. He rebuilt the threshold and the review step within a month and asked me to test it afterwards. That exchange is the reason I agreed to write this.

I would work with him again on any process where the numbers have to be right and the audit trail has to survive scrutiny.

Yours faithfully,
Sadia Rehman
Sadia Rehman · Internal Audit Manager · Nishat Textile Mills
Why this one works
  • The limitation is stated up front. "I am not his manager" — declaring the boundary makes everything inside it more credible.
  • The audit evidence is comparative. "Which was true of nobody else in the department" turns a good outcome into a distinction.
  • One anecdote carries the character judgement. The control weakness and how he responded. A single incident, told precisely, does more than a paragraph of adjectives.
  • The recommendation is bounded. "Any process where the numbers have to be right" — a specific endorsement reads as considered; an unlimited one reads as reflexive.
Sample ThreeWhen your boss says "write it yourself"

The common request in Pakistan and much of the region: your manager agrees, then asks you to draft it. This is what a self-drafted letter should look like — factual, restrained, and easy for them to sign.

Yasir Ahmed
Engineering Manager, Systems Limited  ·  y.ahmed@systemsltd.com  ·  +92 300 8814 227
12 August 2026
To Whom It May Concern
Letter of Recommendation — Mr. Hamza Ali
Dear Sir/Madam,

Hamza Ali has reported to me for three years as a Software Engineer at Systems Limited, from February 2023 to the present.

He rebuilt our core API layer, reducing median response time from 800 milliseconds to 320. He delivered the Stripe payments integration that now processes approximately 15,000 transactions each month, and he took automated test coverage from 34 per cent to 81 per cent, which halved the number of production hotfixes we ship.

He mentors two junior engineers, both of whom reached independent production releases within their first four months, against a team average closer to seven.

In my assessment his particular strength is that he volunteers unglamorous work — test coverage, documentation, on-call runbooks — which is uncommon and disproportionately valuable in a small team.

I recommend him without reservation and can be contacted at the details above.

Yours faithfully,
Yasir Ahmed
Yasir Ahmed · Engineering Manager · Systems Limited
Why this one works
  • It is written in the manager's register, not the candidate's. Measured, slightly reserved, no superlatives. A self-drafted letter that gushes is obvious to any reader.
  • Everything is verifiable. Every claim is a number the manager can confirm in seconds before signing. That is what makes a draft easy to approve.
  • One judgement is offered, and only one. "Volunteers unglamorous work" — the manager can strike it if he disagrees, and if he leaves it, it reads as his.
  • It is short. A manager asked to sign a two-page draft will edit it or delay it. One page gets signed the same day.

Two specifics beat ten adjectives

Read a stack of recommendation letters and they converge on the same vocabulary: hardworking, dedicated, reliable, an asset to any organisation. The words are meant sincerely and they carry no information, because they appear in every letter including the lukewarm ones.

What a reader is actually looking for is evidence that the writer knows this person well enough to say something only they could say. One precise incident does that. Two numbers do it. A qualified judgement — "correct rather than fast" — does it better than any superlative.

A useful test before signing: could this letter be sent about a different person by changing the name? If yes, it has said nothing, however warm it sounds.

The five-paragraph shape

  • One: who you are, how you know them, for how long, and in what capacity. Establishes the weight of everything after.
  • Two: their principal achievement, with the number and the mechanism.
  • Three: a second achievement or an incident that shows character.
  • Four: a specific characterisation — how they work, what they are like to work with, qualified rather than absolute.
  • Five: the recommendation itself, and an offer to be contacted.

One page. A recommendation letter that runs to two pages is usually padding, and padding is read as an absence of substance.

When you are asked to draft it

It is extremely common here for a manager to agree and then ask you to write it. It is not dishonest — they will read, edit and sign it, and their signature is the endorsement. But it is difficult to do well, because the instinct is to write what you wish someone thought of you.

  • Write in their register, not yours. Measured. Slightly reserved. No superlatives.
  • Use only facts they can verify in the ten seconds before signing.
  • Include at most one subjective judgement, and make it easy to strike.
  • Keep it to one page.
  • Send it as an editable document, not a PDF, and say plainly that you expect them to change anything they disagree with.

And attach the same referee brief from your reference set — the role, the dates, the numbers. It gives them the material to add something you would never have thought to include.

The damning ones

Experienced readers know that a lukewarm letter is more informative than a warm one, and they read for the signals.

  • Very short with no specifics. "X worked here from 2022 to 2026 and performed his duties satisfactorily." This is a refusal wearing a letter's clothes.
  • Praise only for reliability and attendance. Punctual, cooperative, never absent — when nothing is said about the work itself, that omission is the message.
  • Careful hedging. "In the time available to him", "given his level of experience".
  • No offer of contact. A writer who will not take a call is telling you something.

If you are asked to write for someone you cannot genuinely recommend, the honest option is to decline. A weak letter damages them more than no letter, and they will not know why.
