Why this one works
  • Money appears. $14,000 a month is $168,000 a year. Senior engineers who can express work in cost terms are read differently by everyone above the engineering manager.
  • p95, not average. Averages hide the users having a bad time. Using p95 signals you already think the way a platform team needs you to.
  • Mean time to recovery is included. Uptime is table stakes; how fast you recover when it breaks is what distinguishes senior operational judgement.
  • Team size and on-call ownership are stated. "Senior" is a title anyone can be given. Five reports and the on-call rota is what it actually means.

Why the stack list does not work

Open twenty developer resumes for the same role and the technology lists will be close to identical. React, Node, Python, SQL, Docker, AWS, Git. The list cannot separate you because it does not vary — and a section that does not vary cannot be a reason to choose anyone.

It is still worth including, because keyword search is real and a recruiter filtering for "Kubernetes" needs the word present. But that is a clerical function, and clerical functions belong at the bottom of the page.

What actually varies between candidates is what they built, how many people used it, what broke, and what happened to the numbers afterwards. That is the top of the page.

How to quantify engineering work

The usual objection is that engineering is not measurable. It is the most measurable work in any office — the measurements are simply in dashboards nobody opens while writing a resume.

  • Latency. Before and after, in milliseconds, and use p95 or p99 rather than average.
  • Throughput. Requests per second, transactions per month, daily active users touched.
  • Reliability. Uptime, error rate, incidents per quarter, mean time to recovery.
  • Cost. Monthly infrastructure spend removed. This one travels furthest up the org chart.
  • Quality. Test coverage, defects reaching QA, hotfixes per release.
  • Delivery. Build time, deploy frequency, lead time from merge to production.
  • People. Engineers mentored, reviews given, runbooks written.

If you truly have no numbers, use scale instead: team size, user count, codebase age, service count. "A four-year-old monolith serving 2.1M users" tells a reader plenty before a single metric appears.

Applying without a degree

A computer science degree is a filter at some companies and irrelevant at many others. The mistake self-taught developers make is not the missing degree — it is trying to disguise it, which produces a resume full of soft language and short on evidence.

The replacement for a degree is shipped work that other people use. Not tutorials completed, not courses watched — deployed things with users. One project with 4,000 real users outweighs fifteen exercises from a curriculum.

Give projects a proper section with a heading, and give each one a number: users, stars, downloads, clients, or revenue. Put your GitHub and a live URL in the header so proof is one click away. And state an incomplete degree plainly — an unexplained gap costs more trust than the incomplete degree ever will.

Four rewrites for engineers

Weak

Worked on improving the performance of the backend API.

Strong

Rebuilt the core API layer, cutting median response time from 800ms to 320ms.

"Worked on" hides whether you led it or watched it. Say what you did and by how much.

Weak

Technologies: React, Node, MongoDB, Express, Docker, AWS, Git, Jira, Agile.

Strong

Shipped a payments integration processing 15,000 transactions monthly.

The first line appears on ten thousand other resumes. The second appears on yours.

Weak

Wrote unit tests to improve code quality.

Strong

Raised automated test coverage from 34% to 81%, halving production hotfixes.

Everyone writes tests. The coverage change and its consequence are what a hiring manager needs.

Weak

Senior developer with strong problem-solving skills and team experience.

Strong

Lead a team of five engineers; own the on-call rota and incident review.

Strong problem-solving is an adjective about yourself. Owning the on-call rota is a fact anyone can verify in a single reference call.
