Set 01 · ATS-Friendly · Resumes

The clean ATS resume, stripped to what parses.

Before a person reads your resume, software reads it. Applicant tracking systems pull your name, your jobs and your dates out of the file and drop them into a database — and they fail on layouts that look perfectly fine to you. These three samples use the format that survives that step, at three career stages.

Why this one works
  • Colour and parsing are not in conflict. An applicant tracking system extracts text; it never sees the ink. This page is as machine-readable as a plain one and considerably harder to forget.

What an ATS actually does

Almost everything written about applicant tracking systems is wrong. They are not artificial intelligence, they do not score your personality, and in most companies they do not reject you automatically. What they do is simpler and more dangerous: they read your file and try to split it into fields — name, email, phone, employer, job title, start date, end date, school, degree.

That is called parsing. When it works, a recruiter searching for "payroll" and "Karachi" finds you. When it fails, your job history lands in the wrong boxes or lands nowhere, and you are simply absent from every search the recruiter runs. Nobody rejected you. You were never there.

So the goal is not to please a robot. It is to be findable by the human who comes after it.

Six things that break it

  • Tables. A two-column table looks tidy and parses as scrambled word order.
  • Text boxes. Many parsers do not read inside them at all — your name can vanish.
  • Headers and footers. Contact details placed there are frequently skipped entirely.
  • Icons instead of labels. A little envelope picture is not the word "Email".
  • Photos. Fine for Gulf applications, useless to the parser, and a distraction elsewhere.
  • Creative date formats. Write "Jan 2022 — Present". Not "'22-now", not a timeline graphic.

Every sample on this page avoids all six. That is the entire reason they look plain. The restraint is the feature, not a shortage of design.

How to test your own in one minute

You do not need special software. Open your resume, select everything, copy it, and paste it into a plain Notepad file.

Read what comes out. If your job titles and dates are in a sensible order and nothing is missing, a parser will manage. If your name is gone, the columns are interleaved, or your employers are separated from their dates — that is exactly what the tracker stores about you.

Do this before every application where you have changed the layout. It takes a minute and it is the only ATS test that matters.

Four rewrites that change everything

Weak

Responsible for managing daily operations of the warehouse facility.

Strong

Ran a 26-staff distribution centre handling 1,400 outbound orders daily.

Responsibility is a job description. What you did with it is a resume.

Weak

Helped improve efficiency and reduce costs across the department.

Strong

Removed PKR 31M in annual cost through route consolidation, with no redundancies.

"Helped improve" could mean anything from leading the project to attending the meeting. Say which, and say by how much.

Weak

Hard-working team player with excellent communication skills.

Strong

HR generalist with six years across recruitment, payroll and employee relations for a 400-person manufacturer.

Nobody has ever been hired for claiming to be hard-working. The second version tells a reader what you are and at what scale, in one line.

Weak

Skills: MS Office, Communication, Teamwork, Leadership

Strong

Skills: Payroll Administration, HRIS Implementation, Pakistan Labour Law, Grievance Handling

The first list is what everyone writes, so it distinguishes nobody. The second is made of the exact words a recruiter types into the search box.
