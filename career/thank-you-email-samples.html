Sample OneSent the same evening

Short, specific, and it references something that was actually said. That last part is what separates it from a template.

Sana Iqbal
sana.iqbal@email.com  ·  +92 302 7741 663
12 August 2026
Ms. Farah Naz Head of Marketing Shan Foods Karachi
Thank you — Marketing Manager interview, 12 August
Dear Ms. Naz,

Thank you for your time this morning. I enjoyed the conversation more than I expected to, particularly the part about why the last agency consolidation stalled.

It has stayed with me. You mentioned that the three remaining agencies each defended their own reporting model, and that measurement never became comparable. When I did something similar we solved that by agreeing a single attribution model before cutting anyone, which was slower to start and removed most of the argument later. I would be glad to talk it through properly if it is useful.

The role is one I want. If there is anything further you need from me, please just ask.

With thanks,
Sana Iqbal
Why this one works
  • It names a specific thing from the conversation. The stalled agency consolidation. This is the whole point — it proves the note was written for her, not pasted.
  • It adds something useful. A short, concrete suggestion about the attribution model. The note earns its place by being worth reading rather than by being polite.
  • It states the want plainly. "The role is one I want." Interviewers frequently cannot tell, and candidates who say so are remembered.
  • It is under 150 words. A long thank you note reads as anxiety.
  • The subject line carries the date. Interviewers see several candidates a day.
Sample TwoOne question went badly

The most valuable use of a thank you note. You get one more attempt at the question you handled poorly, and doing so is read as self-awareness rather than weakness.

Hamza Ali
hamza.ali@email.com  ·  +92 333 9014 662
12 August 2026
Mr. Yasir Ahmed Engineering Manager Careem Karachi
Thank you — Senior Engineer interview, and a better answer
Dear Mr. Ahmed,

Thank you for the interview yesterday. I wanted to follow up on one question I answered poorly.

You asked how I would approach a database migration with no downtime and I gave you a vague answer about replication. The honest answer is that I have not done a zero-downtime migration at your scale. What I have done is a read-replica cutover on a service handling 15,000 transactions a month, where we ran dual writes for eleven days, verified consistency with a reconciliation job, and cut over during a low-traffic window with a tested rollback. It went without incident, but I know that is a much smaller problem than the one you described.

I would rather tell you that than leave you with the impression I gave in the room. The rest of the conversation I enjoyed, particularly the discussion about paying down reliability debt as a standing allocation rather than a project.

With thanks,
Hamza Ali
Why this one works
  • It names the failure directly. "One question I answered poorly." No hedging. This is disarming and almost nobody does it.
  • The better answer is specific and honest about its limits. Dual writes, eleven days, reconciliation job, tested rollback — and then "that is a much smaller problem than yours".
  • The admission is the strongest part. "I have not done this at your scale" turns a weak interview moment into evidence of judgement.
  • It closes on something positive from the interview, so the note does not end on the failure.
Sample ThreeThree interviewers · One note each

Panels need separate notes, each referencing that person's own question. Sending one identical note to three people is worse than sending none, because they compare.

Amna Siddiqui
amna.siddiqui@email.com  ·  +92 321 7719 442
12 August 2026
Mr. Imran Khalid Chief Operating Officer Telenor Pakistan Islamabad
Thank you — Head of Customer Experience panel, 12 August
Dear Mr. Khalid,

Thank you for your time this afternoon, and please pass my thanks to Ms. Baig and Mr. Raza — I am writing to them separately.

Your question was the one I have thought about since: whether cost per contact can keep falling once the easy self-service volume has moved. I do not think it can, and I said so. What I did not say clearly is where I would look next — the second wave is usually in repeat contacts, which are cheaper to remove than they look because they are caused by something upstream. In my last role about a fifth of volume was people calling twice.

I would be glad to go into that further. Thank you again for a genuinely interesting conversation.

With thanks,
Amna Siddiqui
Why this one works
  • It says the other notes are being sent separately. Panels talk to each other, and acknowledging it prevents the note looking like a mass send.
  • It answers his question specifically. Each panel member gets the thread they personally pulled on.
  • It disagrees, politely. "I do not think it can, and I said so." Repeating a disagreement rather than softening it afterwards reads as conviction.
  • It adds the thing she did not say in the room. Repeat contacts as the second wave, with a number attached.

Why it works

The mechanism is unglamorous. Interviewers see several candidates over several days and then discuss them, often a week later, from memory and rough notes. Anything that puts your name and one specific idea back in front of them during that window has a disproportionate effect.

The second reason is that hardly anyone sends one. In most hiring rounds a thank you note arrives from perhaps one candidate in ten, and it is noticed every time — not as flattery, but as evidence of how you follow up on things generally.

And the third: it is your only opportunity to correct something. Interviews are lossy. You forget an example, misunderstand a question, or answer the question you expected rather than the one asked. The note is a second attempt, and taking it is read as self-awareness.

The rules

  • Within 24 hours. Same evening is better. After three days it reads as an afterthought.
  • Email, not a physical letter, unless the organisation is unusually formal.
  • Under 200 words. Length reads as anxiety.
  • Reference something specific that was said. This is non-negotiable — it is the only thing that distinguishes the note from a template.
  • Add one thing of value. A thought, a correction, a link, an answer you improved on.
  • One note per interviewer, each different, if it was a panel.
  • Say you want the job, if you do. Plainly, once.

And do not ask about timelines. The note should cost them nothing to read and require no reply. If you need a timeline, ask at the end of the interview, which is the right moment for it.

Three rewrites

Weak

Thank you for taking the time to meet with me today. I really enjoyed learning more about the role and your company.

Strong

Thank you for your time this morning. I enjoyed the conversation more than I expected to, particularly the part about why the last agency consolidation stalled.

The first could have been sent to any employer on earth. The second could only have been sent to this one.

Weak

I believe my skills and experience make me an excellent fit for this position and I would be a great asset to your team.

Strong

You mentioned the three agencies each defended their own reporting model. When I did something similar we agreed a single attribution model before cutting anyone, which removed most of the argument later.

Assertion, or a useful idea. Only one of them gets forwarded to a colleague.

Weak

Please let me know if you need any additional information. I look forward to hearing about next steps at your earliest convenience.

Strong

The role is one I want. If there is anything further you need from me, please just ask.

The first asks for something. The second offers something and closes cleanly.
