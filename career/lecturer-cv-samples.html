Set 06 · Teaching · CVs

Lecturer CV, for university posts.

A teaching-focused university post is not judged the way a research chair is, and the commonest mistake is sending a research CV with fewer publications on it. What a department needs to know is what you can teach, how many students you can carry, what you have supervised, and what happens to your students. Three samples: a visiting lecturer, a permanent lecturer, and an associate professor on the teaching track.

Why this one works
  • Cumulative student numbers pull the record together. Fourteen sections and 1,100 students across three institutions reads as a substantial teaching load rather than scattered part-time work.
  • Course codes, enrolment and section counts are given. This is what a head of department uses to work out whether you can carry the timetable.
  • PhD status is stated honestly. "Application in progress, admission sought 2027". In Pakistani universities the HEC rules make this decisive, so silence is read as a problem.
  • Prior industry experience is kept as one line. For business teaching it is a genuine asset and it explains the career shape.

Why this one works
  • Graduate employment rate is the strongest line. 71% to 89% within six months is what a programme exists to produce, and it is almost never on an academic CV.
  • Accreditation is stated with the outcome. "Awarded without conditions" separates leading the process from merely being present for it.
  • Both completed and current supervision are shown. Completions prove capability; current load shows a functioning research group.
  • Faculty mentored is a section. Seven junior staff developed is a departmental contribution that a dean weighs when appointing to professor.

Teaching track is not research track

The commonest error in applications for teaching-focused posts is sending a research CV with a shorter publication list. It answers the wrong question and reads as a candidate who wanted a different job.

A department appointing a lecturer is solving a timetable problem and a quality problem. They need to know what you can teach, how many students you can carry, what you can supervise, and whether your students do well. Publications matter — HEC rules and promotion criteria make sure of that — but they are not the argument.

So move teaching above publications, and give it real detail: course codes, enrolments, number of sections, evaluations, courses you designed. A research CV mentions teaching in four lines. A teaching CV spends a page on it.

What Pakistani universities check first

Local academic hiring runs through HEC criteria before anything else, and candidates lose applications on technicalities rather than merit.

  • HEC Approved Supervisor status, and at which level — MS or PhD. This determines whether you can take students, which determines whether the department can appoint you.
  • PhD status and awarding institution, including whether it is HEC-recognised.
  • Publications in HEC-recognised journals, with the category if you know it. A count of papers is not the same as a count of recognised ones.
  • Years of post-PhD teaching experience, since promotion criteria are defined in those terms.
  • PEC or relevant council registration for engineering and professional faculties.

Put all of it in the header or the first block. A committee working through eighty applications against a checklist will not go hunting, and an unanswered checklist item is treated as absent.

Evidence of teaching quality

Everyone applying claims to be a good teacher. Four things can actually evidence it, and most candidates use none of them.

  • Student evaluations. Give the median and the period, not one flattering semester.
  • Courses you designed that are still running, and whether other campuses adopted them.
  • Pass or attainment rates before and after a change you made.
  • Supervision completions and what happened to those students — placements, competitions, publications, employment.

The last one is the strongest and the rarest. "Three projects placed at national student competitions" or "graduate employment rose from 71% to 89%" says something about your teaching that no evaluation score can.

Lead with teaching, and evidence it.
