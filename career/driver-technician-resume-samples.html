The four facts that matter

A supervisor hiring a driver, an electrician or a warehouse worker is looking for four things and will find them in about thirty seconds or move to the next page.

  • Licence or certification. The class, the expiry, and whether the record is clean. This goes in the header, not at the bottom.
  • Record. Accidents, incidents, penalties, shortfalls. Zero is a number and it is the strongest one available in this work.
  • Experience with scale attached. Kilometres, machines, cases, vehicles, team size. The unit tells the reader what size operation you are used to.
  • Availability. Shift patterns you can work, when you can start, whether you have your own vehicle or tools.

Everything else on the page is secondary. If the resume is getting long, cut from elsewhere.

Numbers in practical work

These jobs are counted constantly and the counting is easy to find — it is on the delivery sheet, the shift log, the maintenance record.

  • Volume. Deliveries per day, cases handled, jobs completed, machines maintained.
  • Distance and time. Kilometres driven, response time, restoration time, hours logged.
  • Accuracy. Error rate, stock accuracy, shortfalls, damages.
  • Safety. Incident-free period, injury record, penalties.
  • Money. Cash handled, collections, value of stock or vehicle in your care.
  • People. Team supervised, staff trained.

Round honestly and say "about" if you are estimating. Nobody will hold "about 25 deliveries a day" against you; an invented precise figure that collapses in the interview will cost the job.

Why short is right here

A one-page resume is standard advice everywhere. For this work it is not merely standard, it is the correct answer, and going longer actively hurts.

The person reading it is usually a shift supervisor or transport manager reading between other tasks, often on a phone, often with fifty applications. A long document does not read as thorough — it reads as someone who cannot get to the point, which in this work is itself a signal.

Half a page is fine. Three quarters is common. Fill it with the four facts, the volume numbers and the record, and leave the rest empty. Empty space is not a wasted opportunity; it is what makes the important things findable.
