Set 06 · Application Letters · Cover Letters

Speculative letter, no advertised post.

Writing to a company that is not hiring feels like the weakest possible move and is frequently the strongest, because you are the only letter on the desk. The catch is that a speculative letter has no advertisement to answer, so it must supply its own reason for existing. Three samples: a graduate, an experienced hire, and a return after time away.

Sample OneFresh graduate · Small firm

A small engineering firm with no advertised vacancy. The letter offers something specific and asks for very little.

Hamza Yousaf
hamza.yousaf@email.com  ·  +92 300 7719 224  ·  Lahore, Pakistan  ·  PEC MECH/24-8871
12 August 2026
Mr. Asim Raza Director Raza Engineering Works Badami Bagh, Lahore
Graduate mechanical engineer — introduction
Dear Mr. Raza,

I am not writing about an advertised position. I visited your workshop during a university field visit in March and spoke briefly with your foreman about the gearbox reconditioning work, and it stayed with me.

I graduated this year from UET Lahore with a first-class degree in mechanical engineering and am PEC registered. My final year project was an automated gearbox fatigue test rig, built on a PKR 180,000 budget with a four-member team, which cut test cycle time 34% against the department's manual rig. I also spent six weeks in plant maintenance at Nishat Mills, logging breakdown data across two production lines.

I am aware you may have nothing open. If it would be useful, I would be glad to work for a month unpaid on the reconditioning line so you can see whether I am worth employing. If not, I would still value ten minutes of your time and any advice on where to start.

Yours sincerely,
Hamza Yousaf
Enclosed: resume (1 page) · project report available on request
Why this one works
  • It states immediately that no post is advertised. Pretending otherwise wastes the reader's first four seconds and is transparent.
  • The connection is genuine and checkable. A field visit and a conversation with the foreman. This is what makes it a letter to him rather than a circular.
  • The project is described in the firm's own domain. Gearboxes. He chose the detail that connects to their actual work.
  • The ask is small and escalating. A month unpaid, or ten minutes. Two doors, both easy to open, and the second costs the reader nothing.
Sample TwoEight years · Company you have watched

An experienced candidate approaching a growing company. The letter identifies a problem growth creates and offers to be the answer to it.

Bilal Ahmed
bilal.ahmed@email.com  ·  +92 321 8890 244  ·  Lahore, Pakistan
12 August 2026
Ms. Sadia Kamal Chief Financial Officer Airlift Technologies Lahore
Finance operations — an introduction
Dear Ms. Kamal,

You have opened in four new cities in eighteen months. In my experience that is roughly the point at which month-end close stops working, because the process that suited one entity does not survive four. I am writing in case that is a problem you are currently living with.

I am a senior accountant with six years in month-end close, payables and tax compliance, most recently at Nishat Textile Mills. I own the close for three entities and took it from twelve days to six, largely by standardising intercompany reconciliation rather than adding people. I also recovered PKR 3.2 million in duplicate vendor payments through a reconciliation audit, and have filed three years of returns with no penalties.

I am not asking you to create a position. If close is already under a week, I have misjudged it and will not take more of your time. If it is not, I would be glad to talk about what I would do in the first sixty days.

Yours sincerely,
Bilal Ahmed
Enclosed: resume (1 page)
Why this one works
  • It opens with an informed guess about their problem. Four cities in eighteen months, therefore close is probably broken. Specific enough to be interesting, humble enough to be safe.
  • It gives her an exit. "If close is already under a week, I have misjudged it." This costs nothing and makes the letter feel like a professional courtesy rather than a pitch.
  • The mechanism is named. "By standardising intercompany reconciliation rather than adding people" — a CFO reads that and knows immediately whether he understands the work.
  • The ask is a conversation about sixty days, not a job. It is answerable in one line.
Sample ThreeTwo years out · Re-entering

Returning to work after time away, approaching a former employer's competitor. The break is handled in one sentence and the letter moves on.

Nadia Aslam
nadia.aslam@email.com  ·  +92 321 4405 118  ·  Karachi, Pakistan
12 August 2026
Mr. Ahsan Iqbal Finance Director Packages Convertors Limited Karachi
Finance manager — availability from October
Dear Mr. Iqbal,

I spent four years as finance manager at Nishat Mills and took two years out for family care, during which I completed my remaining ACCA papers. I am returning to full-time work from October and am writing to a small number of companies directly rather than waiting for advertisements.

At Nishat I owned month-end close for three entities and reduced close time from twelve days to six, recovered PKR 3.2 million in duplicate vendor payments through a reconciliation audit, and managed a team of four through an external audit with no material findings. I am now fully ACCA qualified, which I was not when I left.

I know there may be nothing open now. I would be glad to be kept in mind, and if it is more useful I am willing to take a defined project — a reconciliation exercise or an audit preparation — rather than a permanent role to begin with.

Yours sincerely,
Nadia Aslam
Enclosed: resume (1 page)
Why this one works
  • The break is the first sentence and takes eleven words. Raising it immediately removes it as a suspicion; burying it makes it the only thing the reader looks for.
  • The qualification finished during the break. "I am now fully ACCA qualified, which I was not when I left" converts two years of absence into visible progress.
  • A start date is given. October. Speculative letters without a date are hard to act on even when the reader is interested.
  • It offers a smaller shape. A defined project rather than a permanent role is far easier to say yes to, and it is how a large share of returns actually begin.

Why speculative letters work

The instinct is that writing to a company with no vacancy is a waste of a stamp. In practice it has three structural advantages that an application never has.

  • You are the only letter on the desk. An advertised post brings four hundred applications; a speculative letter competes with nothing.
  • Many roles are created rather than advertised, especially in growing companies and family businesses, and especially at senior level.
  • Advertising is expensive and slow. A suitable candidate who arrives before the process starts saves the employer both.

The response rate is low — most letters get nothing. But the cost is fifteen minutes and the occasional reply is a conversation with no competition in it, which is a trade worth making repeatedly.

It must supply its own reason

An application letter borrows its justification from the advertisement. A speculative letter has none, so it must manufacture one in the first paragraph, and this is where almost all of them fail.

Four reasons that work:

  • A genuine connection. A visit, a conversation, a mutual contact, a talk you attended.
  • Something they did. An expansion, an acquisition, a new site, a product launch, a public announcement.
  • An informed guess about their problem. "You have opened four cities in eighteen months, which is usually where close breaks." Risky, and powerful when it lands.
  • A defined offer. A project, a trial period, an unpaid month — something concrete rather than availability.

What does not work: "I have long admired your company", "I am seeking opportunities in your esteemed organisation", or any sentence that could have been sent to two hundred addresses.

Make the ask small

The letter fails if it asks the reader to make a decision, because the easiest decision is no and the second easiest is to not reply at all.

So ask for something that costs almost nothing to grant. Ten minutes. A conversation with no obligation. Being kept on file. A defined short project. An unpaid trial. Advice on who else to write to — which occasionally produces a better introduction than the job you wanted.

And give them a clean exit, explicitly. "If there is nothing, I would still value the conversation" or "If close is already under a week, I have misjudged this." A letter that makes refusal comfortable is far more likely to receive a reply, and a reply is the whole objective.

Write before the advertisement exists.
