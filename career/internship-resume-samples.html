Why this one works

Your CGPA is not the differentiator

Every applicant in the internship pile has a CGPA, and most are within half a point of each other. It is a filter, not an argument — it gets you into the pile and does nothing once you are there.

What separates applications is evidence of having done something with your own hands and being able to describe it precisely. A project with a budget. A society role with a number. A tutoring client. A competition with a field size. Something that happened in the world rather than only on a transcript.

The bar is far lower than students assume. Supervisors are not expecting professional output from a second-year. They are checking whether you finish things, whether you can explain them, and whether you will turn up.

How to write a coursework project

A project title is not a project. Write four things about each one and it becomes evidence:

  • What it was and what it did. One clear sentence, no jargon from the course.
  • Constraints. Budget, team size, timeframe, equipment available.
  • Your role specifically. If it was a team, say what you personally did.
  • The outcome. Grade, test results, whether it worked, whether it was selected, demonstrated or reused.

And be honest about scope. A PKR 9,000 solar controller from a second-year student is entirely respectable. The same project described as "designed and implemented a renewable energy management system" is not more impressive — it is less believable, and it will not survive one technical question.

Applying, practically

A few things that decide internship applications and are rarely mentioned.

  • State expected graduation year. Programmes are structured around it and it is often a hard filter.
  • State exact availability dates. "June — August 2026" not "summer".
  • Apply early. Most Pakistani internship intakes fill in February and March for summer. A strong application in May competes for whatever is left.
  • Put your GitHub, portfolio or LinkedIn in the header if you have one worth opening — and only if it is worth opening.
  • One page, always. There is no version of a student resume that needs two.
  • Name the department or team you want if the organisation has several. Specificity reads as genuine interest, and mass applications are obvious.

Small and specific beats large and vague.
