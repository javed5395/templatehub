The Contributor Program is Live: sell your templates on LazyDog

Until today, every template on LazyDogTemplates was made in-house. That changes now. The Contributor Program is open — independent designers can upload their own work to the store, set their own prices, and keep up to 80% of every sale. No listing fee, no monthly charge, no exclusivity. This post explains what the programme is, and walks you through joining it step by step.

What the Contributor Program is

It is a simple arrangement. You design the template. We host it, list it, handle the storefront, take the payment, deliver the file to the buyer, and send you your share. You are not an employee and you are not signing anything binding — you can stop at any time.

We are looking for pitch decks, media kits, résumés and cover letters, web kits, and keynote decks. If your work fits one of those sections, it fits the store.

What you get

  • Up to 80% of every sale. On any template priced above $5, the site takes 20% and you keep 80%. At $5 or below, the site takes 25%.
  • Payment fees are on us. Card fees, processing charges, currency conversion — the site absorbs all of it. Your percentage is calculated on the full sale price, not on what's left after fees.
  • You set both prices. A personal-use price and a commercial-licence price. Both are yours to decide.
  • Your own earnings page. Every sale appears in your dashboard — what it sold for, what the site took, and exactly what you earned.
  • Optional marketer commission. If you want affiliates promoting your template, you set the rate yourself, anywhere from 0% to 40%.
  • Nothing to pay, ever. No upload fee, no subscription, no minimum number of templates.
  • Not exclusive. Sell the same work elsewhere if you want to. We don't lock you in.

How the money splits

Here is the whole thing on one line — a template that sells for $20, with no marketer involved:

Sale priceSite shareYou keep
$20.0020% — $4.00$16.00
$4.0025% — $1.00$3.00

If you choose to offer a marketer commission, that percentage comes out of your share, not the site's — so you always know your floor before you set it. Sell that same $20 template through a marketer on 30%, and it looks like this: site takes $4, marketer takes $6, you keep $10.

Earnings are held for 20 days after each sale — this is the refund and dispute window, and it protects both of us. After that the money moves to available, and once your available balance reaches $50 you can be paid out.

How to become a contributor — step by step

The whole thing happens on the top bar of the site. Watch that one button — it changes as you move through the process:

LazyDog Templates Become a Contributor → Application under review → Upload

  1. Sign in to the site

    Use the sign-in option on the top bar. A free account is all you need — the same account a customer would use. Verify your email when the message arrives, because the next step requires it.

  2. Click Become a Contributor on the top bar

    Once you are signed in, this button appears on the navigation bar at the top of every page. Clicking it opens the application form.

  3. Fill in the application form

    It is short. Your name, your contact email, a link to your portfolio or previous work, the payout method you'd like to be paid through, and one photo of a government ID so we know who we're paying.

    The ID photo is used for that one check only, and it is deleted automatically the moment your application is decided. We do not keep it.

  4. Press Submit and wait for review

    Your application goes straight to our team. While it is being looked at, the top-bar button changes to Application under review — so you always know where you stand without having to email anyone.

  5. Get approved — the button becomes Upload

    When we approve you, that same button on the top bar turns into Upload automatically. Nothing to install, no second login, no separate portal address to remember.

  6. Click Upload and add your first template

    The upload form asks for your design file, a title, a description, a category, your personal-use price, your commercial-licence price, and — if you want one — a marketer commission rate. It shows you the arithmetic live as you type, so you can see your earnings on each sale before you publish.

  7. Track your sales on the Earnings page

    Once your template is live, every sale lands on your Earnings page: what it sold for, what the site took, what any marketer took, and what you earned. Once your available balance passes $50, request your payout.

What we look for when reviewing

We are not fussy, but we are careful. We check three things: that the work is genuinely yours, that the file is complete and actually usable by a buyer, and that the presentation is good enough to sit next to everything else in the store. If something isn't right we will tell you what to change rather than simply rejecting you — this is early days, and we would much rather help you get listed than turn you away.

A few honest notes

  • We are new. The store is young and traffic is still building. Nobody is going to get rich here this month. If you are looking for an established marketplace with millions of buyers, we are not that — yet.
  • That is also the opportunity. Early contributors get a near-empty shelf and first pick of every category.
  • Payouts go to your own account. You'll be paid through the payout method you choose in the application. Opening the account is free and takes a couple of minutes.
  • Refunds work both ways. If a buyer refunds or disputes a sale within the 20-day window, that earning is reversed. This is why the hold exists.
Read the full terms before you apply
Every rule above — the split, the hold, the payout threshold, ownership of your work — is written out plainly on the contributor terms page. No fine print, no surprises.
