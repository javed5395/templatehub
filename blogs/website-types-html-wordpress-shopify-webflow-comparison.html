HTML vs WordPress vs Shopify vs Webflow — Which Website Type Is Best?

LD LazyDog Team  ·  30 Aug 2026
LazyDogTemplates  |  LazyDog Studios
← Back to Journal

Everyone building a website hits the same wall early: which platform do I use? The options sound similar at first — HTML, WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Wix, Squarespace — but they are built for completely different situations, different budgets, and different levels of technical comfort. Pick the wrong one and you spend the next two years fighting limitations that were obvious from day one. This guide cuts through the noise.

Why the Choice Actually Matters

The platform you build on controls three things that are very difficult to change later: how fast your site loads, how much it costs to run every year, and how easily you can update it. A wrong call on any one of these compounds over time. Speed affects your Google ranking. Cost affects your margin. Editing difficulty affects how often your content actually gets updated — which affects everything else.

Here is a complete breakdown of every major platform, starting with the most fundamental choice: building in plain HTML.

Option 01
Plain HTML / CSS Website

A website written directly in HTML and CSS, with no content management system behind it. The files are exactly what the browser reads — there is no database, no login, no server-side code doing anything at runtime. Hosting is either free (GitHub Pages, Netlify) or very cheap.

✓ Advantages
  • Fastest possible load speed — no database calls, no server-side processing
  • Zero or near-zero hosting cost (GitHub Pages is free)
  • No plugins, no updates, no security patches to chase
  • Nothing to be hacked at the CMS level — no login page exists
  • Full control over every pixel and every line of code
  • No licence fees, no annual subscriptions to any platform
  • Works offline, works on any hosting provider, never ties you to a vendor
  • Excellent SEO — clean markup, fast load, no bloat
✗ Disadvantages
  • Editing content means editing files — not beginner-friendly
  • Adding new pages or blog posts requires writing HTML
  • No built-in CMS — no dashboard for non-technical staff
  • Scaling to hundreds of pages becomes repetitive without a build tool
  • Dynamic features (forms, e-commerce) need third-party services
Annual cost: $10–15 (domain only). Hosting is free on GitHub Pages or Netlify.
Best for: Portfolios, business brochure sites, landing pages, template-based sites that change infrequently — and anyone who values speed and low cost above everything else.
Option 02
WordPress

The world's most popular CMS, powering about 43% of all websites. Content lives in a database; pages are assembled on the server when someone visits. Non-technical staff can add posts and pages through a dashboard without touching code. The trade-off is a significant ongoing maintenance overhead.

✓ Advantages
  • Anyone can add blog posts, pages and media without a developer
  • Massive plugin ecosystem — almost any feature already exists
  • Huge community, thousands of themes, huge support network
  • Scales well for blogs, news sites and content-heavy platforms
  • WooCommerce turns it into a capable online store
  • Relatively low hosting cost compared to hosted platforms
✗ Disadvantages
  • Regular updates required — WordPress core, themes, and every plugin
  • Plugins conflict, break, or get abandoned — a constant management task
  • Slower than HTML by default — needs caching plugins and optimisation
  • Most targeted CMS for hacking — login pages and plugins are attack surfaces
  • Real annual cost is higher than it looks once hosting + theme + plugins add up
  • Performance degrades as plugins accumulate
Annual cost: $100–$500+ depending on hosting quality, premium theme licence, and paid plugins.
Best for: Blogs, news sites, content-heavy platforms where non-technical staff publish regularly — typically more than a few times per week.
Option 03
Shopify

A hosted e-commerce platform built specifically for selling products online. Everything a store needs — payments, inventory, shipping, discounts, abandoned cart emails — is built in or available through its app store. You do not manage a server; Shopify handles all of that.

✓ Advantages
  • Built specifically for selling — payments, inventory and shipping all work out of the box
  • Handles PCI compliance and security for card payments
  • Scales from one product to hundreds of thousands with no infrastructure work
  • Excellent app ecosystem for marketing, reviews, loyalty, subscriptions
  • Reliable uptime — hosted infrastructure, no server to manage
  • Strong analytics and reporting built in
✗ Disadvantages
  • Monthly subscription is mandatory — you cannot own the platform
  • Transaction fees on every sale unless you use Shopify Payments
  • Limited design freedom compared to HTML or Webflow
  • Content and blog features are basic — not built for content marketing
  • App costs stack up quickly for anything beyond basics
  • Leaving Shopify means rebuilding from scratch
Annual cost: $360–$2,400+ (Basic to Advanced plans), plus transaction fees and app subscriptions.
Best for: Product-based businesses selling physical or digital goods — from small boutiques to large catalogues. Not ideal for pure content or service businesses.
Option 04
Webflow

A visual design tool that generates clean HTML and CSS as you design. Sits between a website builder and a full development environment. Designers with no code knowledge can build complex layouts; developers can access the underlying code. Hosted by Webflow.

✓ Advantages
  • Visual design with pixel-level control — the closest to design tools like Figma
  • Outputs clean, production-ready HTML and CSS
  • Built-in CMS for content updates without a developer
  • Animations and interactions are easy to build visually
  • Strong for agency and studio sites that need to look exceptional
  • No plugin conflicts — everything is one platform
✗ Disadvantages
  • Learning curve is steep — significantly harder than Wix or Squarespace
  • Monthly cost is higher than WordPress self-hosted for similar features
  • E-commerce is limited compared to Shopify
  • Content locked in Webflow's CMS — migration is difficult
  • Not suited to very large sites or complex databases
Annual cost: $180–$500+ depending on plan and number of CMS items or e-commerce orders.
Best for: Design-led agencies, studios, and businesses where visual polish is a core part of the brand, and a designer (not a developer) is building the site.
Option 05
Wix

A fully hosted drag-and-drop website builder aimed at complete beginners. No code required. Drag elements anywhere on the page. Hundreds of templates. Everything — hosting, domain, SSL — is bundled into one monthly price.

✓ Advantages
  • Genuinely beginner-friendly — no technical knowledge needed at all
  • Fast to set up — a working site in one afternoon
  • Hosting, SSL, and basic tools all included in one price
  • Good template library for small businesses and personal sites
  • AI assistant can generate a first draft automatically
✗ Disadvantages
  • Cannot switch templates once a site is live — a redesign means rebuilding
  • Sites are slower than HTML or well-optimised WordPress
  • Limited SEO control — important tags and settings are buried or missing
  • Free tier shows Wix branding — not suitable for a professional business
  • Cannot export the site — your content is locked to Wix forever
  • Design freedom is less than Webflow despite the visual editor
Annual cost: $170–$550 depending on plan. Free tier available but not suitable for business use.
Best for: Solo freelancers, small local businesses, event pages, and anyone who needs a simple web presence quickly with zero technical involvement.
Option 06
Squarespace

A hosted website builder with a stronger design focus than Wix. Templates are fewer but more polished. Popular with photographers, artists, and creative professionals. Editing is less freeform than Wix but the output tends to look more considered.

✓ Advantages
  • Polished, design-quality templates — among the best of any builder
  • Good built-in e-commerce for small stores
  • Clean, simple editing experience once you know the system
  • Good portfolio and gallery features for creative professionals
  • All-in-one pricing — no surprise add-on costs
✗ Disadvantages
  • Less flexible than Webflow for custom layouts and interactions
  • Plugin/extension ecosystem is small compared to WordPress
  • Content locked in — exporting is limited
  • Not built for heavy content or large blogs
  • SEO features are decent but not as granular as WordPress or HTML
Annual cost: $192–$480 depending on plan. No free tier — trial only.
Best for: Photographers, artists, small product shops, and service businesses where design quality matters and the owner wants to manage it without a developer.

The Honest Verdict — Who Should Use What

There is no single best platform. There is only the right match between what you need and what a platform actually delivers. Here is the straight answer by situation:

You want a fast, low-cost business site
→ Plain HTML
Cheapest to run, fastest to load, best for SEO. Works perfectly for business brochure sites, portfolios, landing pages, and any site that does not change daily. Use a professional HTML template and the maintenance burden nearly disappears.
You publish content every week
→ WordPress
The CMS dashboard is what justifies WordPress. If your team needs to publish blog posts, news, or updates regularly without touching code, WordPress is the right call. Invest in good hosting and keep plugins minimal.
You sell products online
→ Shopify
Nothing beats Shopify for e-commerce. Payments, inventory, shipping, and cart recovery all work on day one. The monthly cost is real, but the alternative — building all of that yourself — is far more expensive.
You are a designer building client sites
→ Webflow
Webflow lets designers produce professional, fully custom sites without relying on a developer. The output is clean code, the CMS is genuinely good, and the result looks like it cost far more than it did.
You need something live this week, no tech skills
→ Wix or Squarespace
Both get you online fast with no technical knowledge. Squarespace if design quality matters most. Wix if you want maximum drag-and-drop flexibility. Accept the limitations and move on — they are real tools, not toys.
You want total long-term control
→ Plain HTML
Every other platform owns something — your content, your data, your layout, your pricing. HTML is yours completely. Move it to any host in minutes. Never pay a licence fee. Never be held hostage by a platform shutdown or a price increase.

One Thing Worth Saying Plainly

The web is full of articles that recommend the platform their affiliate link pays the most for. The honest answer to "which is best" is almost always: HTML for simplicity, WordPress for content volume, Shopify for selling. Most small and medium businesses — the kind that update their site a few times a year and care most about looking professional and loading fast — are better served by a well-built HTML site than by any CMS platform. They end up paying for WordPress's editing capability and never using it.

If you are reading this and building a business website that does not need daily content updates, save the subscription fees, use a professional HTML template, and put the money into marketing instead.
